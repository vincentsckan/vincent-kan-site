## The Cold War UFO Race

During the Cold War, UFOs were not just a civilian topic but also:

1. **Military Threat**: Both sides feared the other was using "UFOs" as cover for reconnaissance aircraft
2. **Intelligence Gathering**: Both sides had dedicated departments collecting UFO intelligence
3. **Psychological Warfare**: Using UFO phenomena to disrupt the opponent

## Soviet UFO Archives

Although the Soviet Union publicly denied the existence of UFOs, it maintained detailed internal UFO investigation records:

- **Ministry of Defense UFO Working Group**: Established in the 1970s, known as the "Grid" program
- **Famous Case**: The Petrozavodsk phenomenon (1977) — a jellyfish-shaped luminous object enveloping the city's sky
- **KGB Role**: The KGB also collected UFO intelligence

## U.S. Military Response Mechanisms

During the Cold War, the U.S. military deployed:
- Continuous UFO monitoring by the North American Aerospace Defense Command (NORAD)
- Alert procedures by the Strategic Air Command (SAC)
- UFO sighting records at nuclear bases (e.g., the 1967 Malmstrom Air Force Base incident)

## Chinese UFO Records

China also has a rich history of UFO records:

- **Ancient Records**: The "Song Shi" (History of Song) documents "heavenly fire" and "stellar anomalies"
- **1960s-1970s**: Numerous civilian sighting reports
- **Official Archives**: The Chinese Academy of Sciences once conducted internal UFO investigations

## Declassified Archives

With the end of the Cold War, some UFO-related military archives have begun to be declassified:

- **U.S. CIA**: Over 2,700 pages of UFO-related files
- **UK MOD**: "UFO files" have been gradually released
- **Russia**: Some Soviet archives have been declassified but not fully disclosed
- **China**: The lowest level of openness

## Modern Revival

During the Russia-Ukraine war, both sides have reported mysterious drones and unidentified flying objects, reminiscent of the Cold War-era UFO military rivalry.
