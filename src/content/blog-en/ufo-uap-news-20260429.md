### 🔥 Schumer-Rounds Submit Independent UAP Disclosure Bill — Bypassing NDAA for Direct Legislation

**Source:** Congress.gov, Politico, The Debrief (April 21–28, 2026)

Senate Majority Leader **Chuck Schumer (D-NY)** and Senator **Mike Rounds (R-SD)** formally introduced the **UAP Disclosure Act of 2026 (S. 4521)** on April 21 as a standalone bill, bypassing the frequently obstructed National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) framework.

**📋 Core Bill Content (36 pages):**

| Provision | Details |
|------|-------|
| **Mandatory Declassification** | Requires all federal agencies (DoD, CIA, FBI, etc.) to review and declassify UAP records within 270 days |
| **Independent Review Board** | Establishes a 9-member independent commission appointed by the President and congressional leaders, with authority to overturn classification decisions |
| **Covers Private Entities** | Mandates disclosure of UAP materials held by contractors (e.g., Lockheed Martin), explicitly covering "non-human intelligence" evidence |
| **Penalties** | Non-compliance results in fines/imprisonment; prohibits destruction of records |
| **Timeline** | Archive opens January 1, 2027; target full public release by December 31, 2028 |

**🗣️ Schumer on the Senate floor (April 21):**
> *"The American people deserve the truth... we have waited too long for half-measures."*

**🗣️ Rounds emphasizes national security:**
Noted that since 2021, AARO has received over 800 UAP reports, 21 of which exhibit "anomalous characteristics."

**⚠️ Significant Opposition:**
- During Senate debate on April 25, Minority Leader **Mitch McConnell (R-KY)** invoked procedural moves to block Sections 1842 (public disclosure timeline) and 1843 (civilian oversight), citing "excessive executive power" and "harm to intelligence sources"
- The revised NDAA version has removed core disclosure provisions, retaining only vague reporting requirements
- Schumer has gathered **45 co-sponsors**, vowing to push forward via standalone legislation
- In the House, Rep. Burchett and Rep. Luna have introduced a companion bill, H.R. 1187

**🔮 Outlook:** The bill needs 60 votes to pass the Senate. Schumer hopes to attach it to an upcoming must-pass bill. On April 28, the Senate sent the NDAA to conference committee with a 99-1 vote; the fate of UAP provisions remains uncertain.

**🔍 Key Case: 1967 Malmstrom Air Force Base, Montana Incident**
- Multiple former Air Force personnel reported: after a luminous object appeared over the base, several **Minuteman intercontinental ballistic missiles** simultaneously became inoperable
- The timing of the missile navigation system failures **perfectly coincided** with the UAP sighting
- This is the most famous among several similar Cold War incidents

**🗣️ Knuth's Position:**

**⚠️ Official Stance:**
- The U.S. Department of Defense and AARO state such claims lack verification

- Knuth himself emphasizes prioritizing conventional explanations, noting that while UAPs' extreme speeds and accelerations challenge known technology, they do not necessarily imply extraterrestrial involvement
